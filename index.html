How this decides what is a note and what is an overtone

A plain FFT will not tell you. A single low C on a piano puts strong energy on C, C, G, C, E, G, B♭ … so a raw spectrum reports a dominant 7th chord that nobody played.

Harmonograph instead builds a dictionary: one idealised column per piano key, each column containing that key's whole harmonic series with geometrically decaying partial amplitudes. It then solves a non-negative least squares fit — which combination of 88 keys, at what strengths, best reconstructs the spectrum you actually have? Energy at 3f gets charged to the key at f instead of being reported as its own note. This is the NNLS approximate-transcription idea from Mauch & Dixon (ISMIR 2010), the front end of Chordino.

Real % is then computed per key: of the energy sitting on that key's fundamental, how much is its own versus how much is explained as a partial of a lower key that is also present. A low score means you are probably looking at a ghost.

Everything runs on your device. Nothing is uploaded.
